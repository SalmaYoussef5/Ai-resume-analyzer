A strong resume should start with a concise professional summary (2-3 sentences) that highlights the candidate's role, years of experience, and key strengths, tailored to the specific job being applied for.

Resumes should quantify achievements whenever possible. Instead of writing "worked on backend features," a stronger version would be "built 5 REST API endpoints that reduced response time by 30%."

Technical skills should be grouped clearly (e.g., Languages, Frameworks, Databases, Tools) rather than listed as one long unorganized line, making it easier for recruiters and automated screening systems to scan.

Work experience should be listed in reverse chronological order, with the most recent role first, and should focus on impact and results rather than just listing daily responsibilities.

For candidates with limited work experience, academic projects, internships, freelance work, and personal open-source contributions are valuable substitutes that should be highlighted prominently.

A resume should ideally fit on one page for candidates with less than 10 years of experience, using clear formatting, consistent fonts, and enough white space to remain readable.

Including a link to a GitHub profile, portfolio, or LinkedIn is highly recommended for technical roles, as it gives recruiters concrete evidence of practical skills beyond the resume text.

Avoid generic filler phrases like "hard-working team player" without supporting evidence; instead, back up soft skills with brief examples from real experience.

Tailoring a resume to each job description - by mirroring some of the key skills and terminology mentioned in the posting - significantly increases the chances of passing automated applicant tracking systems (ATS).
